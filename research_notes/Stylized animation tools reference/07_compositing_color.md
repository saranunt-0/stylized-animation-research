## Q1. What compositing/post approaches did Arcane, Project Gold, and Mielgo's films use?

### Takeaway
All three get most of their "painterly" look **upstream**, through painted textures, painted backgrounds and 3D brushstroke geometry. Compositing then integrates the layers and adds light effects, grading and texture overlays. It is not a single "paint filter". Fortiche's compositing department in Nuke produced Arcane's final images and handled lighting, effects and the "artistic rendering". Mielgo paints backgrounds by hand, puts 3D-animated characters into them, and finishes in After Effects with exposure/level work. Project Gold's painterly look comes from a Geometry-Nodes **Brushstroke Tools** add-on plus Cycles NPR shading and an extended viewport compositor, not from a post filter.

### Inferences
- For a Blender/open-source recreation, the evidence points to **painted inputs plus a light comp** rather than a heavy per-frame "paint filter". That means projected or painted textures, 2D backplates, and 3D brushstroke geometry (Project Gold), followed by glows, grading, grain and texture overlays in comp. Kuwahara-type filters are a finishing or unifying tool, not the source of the look. [U]
- Mielgo's "play with the levels / overexposure" note suggests the comp-time exposure, bloom and halation decisions are central to his look, and that it can be imitated with Glare (Bloom/Fog Glow) plus a highlight-rolloff grade. [U]

## Q2. Which free/open-source tools can deliver a painterly finishing pass (Kuwahara, strokes, paper), and what are the quality / temporal-stability trade-offs?

### Takeaway
The **Blender compositor (4.2+ GPU, 5.x node-group/asset-based)** is the strongest free option. It has native Kuwahara (classic + anisotropic, with a per-pixel size input), Glare (Bloom, Fog Glow, Streaks, Sun Beams…), edge-detect filters, Posterize, lens dispersion, Defocus, Cryptomatte, light-group passes and an in-graph Convert to Display. **G'MIC** (CeCILL) adds the richest set of painterly filters (Brushify, Painting, Paint With Brush, Ink Wash, Canvas/Paper Texture, Add Grain) for GIMP/Krita/CLI, with an old, beta OFX port. **Natron** (official) has had no new numbered release since 2.5.0; an unofficial Qt6 fork shipped 2.6 builds in Sept 2026. Image-space filters avoid the "shower-door" texture lock but can **shimmer** wherever input noise or fine detail changes between frames. EbSynth-style patch propagation looks the most hand-painted, but needs keyframes and fights flicker and sliding.

### Cited Findings

**A. Compositing software (free first)**
- **Blender Compositor**, documentation.
  - **Kuwahara node** (added in **Blender 4.0**: the source file exists in `blender-v4.0-release` and not in 3.6) [V] ([4.0 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v4.0-release/source/blender/nodes/composite/nodes/node_composite_kuwahara.cc)). Its description is "Apply smoothing filter that preserves edges, for stylized and painterly effects".
    - Inputs: **Size** (px, default 6, read **per pixel**, so a depth or mask image can vary stroke size), **Type** Classic/Anisotropic (default Anisotropic), **Uniformity** (default 4), **Sharpness** (0–1), **Eccentricity** (0–2; "maximally directed along the edges"), **High Precision** ("use if the output contains undesirable noise") [V] ([main source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/main/source/blender/nodes/composite/nodes/node_composite_kuwahara.cc)).
    - The anisotropic mode implements Kyprianidis et al., "Anisotropic Kuwahara Filtering with Polynomial Weighting Functions" [V] (same file).
    - The manual says large sizes "may introduce artifacts for highly detailed areas" and anisotropic is slower [S] ([Manual 5.2 LTS: Kuwahara](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/creative/kuwahara.html); [Manual 5.0 path](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/compositing/types/filter/kuwahara.html)).
    - The GPU anisotropic version is "a higher quality one that is also an order of magnitude faster" than the CPU one [S] ([PR #110786](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/110786); [PR #109292 classic](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/109292); [PR #107015 original node](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/107015); [devtalk feedback thread](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/compositor-new-node-kuwahara-filter/29205); [4.0 VFX release notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.0/vfx/); [BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2023/09/04/kuwahara-real-time-paint-filter-in-blender-4-0/)).
  - **GPU compositing for final renders**: the scene property `compositor_device` (CPU/GPU, "Set how compositing is executed") first appears in **4.2** (absent in 4.1). There are also `compositor_precision` and `compositor_denoise_device` settings [V] ([4.2/5.2 rna_scene.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.2-release/source/blender/makesrna/intern/rna_scene.cc)).
  - **Glare node** types: Bloom, Ghosts, Streaks, Fog Glow, Simple Star, **Sun Beams**, **Kernel** (convolve your own kernel). Controls include highlight Threshold/Smoothness, Clamp/Maximum highlights, Strength, Saturation, **Tint**, Size, and Color Modulation ("spectral dispersion") [V] ([glare source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/main/source/blender/nodes/composite/nodes/node_composite_glare.cc)). The Sun Beams node was merged into Glare in 5.0 [V] (`sunbeams` node file removed between 4.5 and 5.0; Glare has SUN_BEAMS in 5.0) [S] ([5.0 compositor notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/compositor/)).
  - **Filter node** (for ink lines/edges): Soften, Box/Diamond Sharpen, **Laplace, Sobel, Prewitt, Kirsch**, Shadow [V] ([filter source 5.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.2-release/source/blender/nodes/composite/nodes/node_composite_filter.cc)).
  - **Lens Distortion** has a **Dispersion** input: "The amount of chromatic aberration" [V] ([source 5.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.2-release/source/blender/nodes/composite/nodes/node_composite_lens_distortion.cc)). **Posterize** takes Steps 2–1024 (default 8) [V].
  - Node list in **5.2 LTS** includes kuwahara, posterize, glare, defocus, bokeh_blur, convolve, **mask_to_sdf**, pixelate, dilate_erode, inpaint, denoise, cryptomatte, depth_combine, convert_to_display, convert_color_space, string_to_image, blank_image [V] ([5.2 CMakeLists](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.2-release/source/blender/nodes/composite/CMakeLists.txt)).
    - Between 4.5 and 5.0, **Composite** (replaced by Group Output), Math, MixRGB, Map Range, Value, Texture and Sun Beams node files were removed, and convert_to_display, convolve, depth_combine and group_output were added [V]. This matches "Texture node" migration notes [S] ([5.0 migration](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/migration/compositor_migration/); [b3d.interplanety on 5.0 API changes](https://b3d.interplanety.org/en/changes-in-node-tree-creation-for-compositor-in-blender-5-0/)).
  - Blender **5.0**: the compositing node tree became its own **data-block**, reusable across files. An **Asset Shelf** in the node editor reuses node groups, and new bundled **compositing assets/utility node groups** give starting looks. New: Convolve node; Alpha Over Disjoint/Conjoint; Split rotation; Displace interpolation [S] ([5.0 compositor notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/compositor/); [CG Channel 5.0](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/11/blender-5-0-is-out-check-out-its-5-key-features/); [CG Cookie 5.0](https://blog.cgcookie.com/posts/everything-new-in-blender-5-0/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/major-compositor-improvements-coming-in-blender-5-0); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/11/20/blender-5-0-its-here/)). ### Inferences
- **Temporal stability ranking (best to worst), [U]:**
  1. 3D-space strokes (Project Gold-style geometry): stable, because strokes are attached to the surface.
  2. Image-space edge-preserving filters (Kuwahara, Filter, Posterize) on **clean, denoised** passes: no texture lock, but they shimmer where noise or sub-pixel detail changes. Denoise first; use High Precision if noisy [V-desc]; lower Size and raise Uniformity for steadier orientation.
  3. Patch propagation (EbSynth family): the most "hand-painted", but it drifts or flickers between keyframes unless you use temporal NNF, bidirectional blending, or frequent keyframes. The ReEzSynth README's feature list implies these are known failure modes.
  4. Per-frame neural style transfer without temporal losses: worst flicker. That is why "artistic-videos" and "ReReVST" exist.
- **Static paper/canvas overlays** create a "shower-door" look (the texture stays fixed while objects move) [U]. Common mitigations are slow drift or boiling the texture on 2s/3s, or UV/triplanar-projected paper in the shader instead of the comp.
- Driving Kuwahara **Size** with a per-pixel map (depth, Cryptomatte mask, or SDF from Mask to SDF) is a cheap way to get Arcane/Mielgo-like "detail where it matters, abstraction elsewhere" [U, based on the verified per-pixel Size input].
- Licensing: for commercial work, prefer public-domain Ebsynth core, MIT ReEzSynth/ComfyUI-EbSynth, MIT AdaIN and Apache-2.0 CCPL. Avoid "non-profit only" artistic-videos and the Snap-encumbered Few-Shot repo. For diffusion-based tools, the model weights' licenses and training data are separate questions. The Arcane AI-poster backlash shows reputational risk [S/U].

## Q3. Which color pipeline (AgX / Standard / OCIO configs) suits stylized NPR rendering in Blender as of 2026?

### Takeaway
Blender 5.x ships one OCIO v2 config with **Standard, AgX (+Punchy/contrast looks), Khronos PBR Neutral, Filmic, ACES 1.3 and ACES 2.0** views, plus HDR displays. New scenes default to **AgX**. For **toon/flat-color NPR** where painted albedo colors must land on screen unchanged, use **Standard**, because it applies only the display encoding. Use AgX or ACES 2.0 for painterly-but-lit looks with bright highlights that must roll off and desaturate gracefully. Grade in Blender's compositor (Convert to Display is now a node) or in Resolve, using the same OCIO/AgX DCTL.

### Cited Findings
- **Views shipped**:
  - **Blender 4.2 and 4.5** config `active_views`: Standard, Khronos PBR Neutral, AgX, Filmic, Filmic Log, False Color, Raw [V] ([4.5 config.ocio](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v4.5-release/release/datafiles/colormanagement/config.ocio)).
  - **Blender 5.0+**: ACES 1.3 and **ACES 2.0** views (SDR and HDR 500–4000-nit), AgX HDR 1000-nit/SDR, and Display P3, Rec.1886, Rec.2020, Rec.2100-PQ/HLG displays; OCIO profile 2.4 (5.0) and 2.5 (5.2); scene_linear role still **Linear Rec.709** [V] ([5.0 config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.0-release/release/datafiles/colormanagement/config.ocio); [5.2 config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.2-release/release/datafiles/colormanagement/config.ocio)).
  - The 5.x config also lists working/interchange spaces including **ACEScg**, ACES2065-1, Linear Rec.2020, DCI-P3, FilmLight E-Gamut, and camera logs (ARRI LogC3/4, Apple Log, BMD, DaVinci Intermediate, RED, V-Log, etc.) [V] (same file).
- **Standard** is defined as "Apply only the display's standard transform without additional mappings" (no tone mapping). **AgX Base** uses a log2 allocation and a 3D LUT (`AgX_Base_sRGB.cube`). AgX/False Color/ACES **Looks** include AgX – Punchy, Greyscale, Very High…Very Low Contrast, and ACES Reference Gamut Compression [V] ([main config.ocio](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/main/release/datafiles/colormanagement/config.ocio)).
- **Defaults**: new scenes are set to `"AgX"` ("Default to AgX view transform"). The Video Editing and 2D Animation templates use **Standard** ("Filmic is too slow, use standard until it is optimized") [V] ([versioning_defaults.cc, 5.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.2-release/source/blender/blenloader/intern/versioning_defaults.cc)).
- **AgX origin**: Eary Chow's AgX OCIO v2 config was "merged to Blender 4.0 main" as a replacement for Filmic. It fixes Filmic's "Notorious Six" hue collapse and gives "smooth chromatic attenuation" for wide-gamut data [V] ([EaryChow/AgX](https://github.com/EaryChow/AgX)) (documentation). Theory and generator: [sobotka/AgX](https://github.com/sobotka/AgX) (links to AgX-S2O3 and SB2383 config generation) [V]. A Resolve DCTL port matches Blender AgX for grading outside Blender ([EaryChow/Blender-AgX-Resolve](https://github.com/EaryChow/Blender-AgX-Resolve)) [V].
- **Convert to Display node** (5.0+): "Convert from scene linear to display color space, with a view transform and look for tone mapping". This lets you place the view transform *inside* the comp graph, so display-referred stylization like posterize, palette remap or paper multiply can follow it [V] ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender/blender-v5.2-release/source/blender/nodes/composite/nodes/node_composite_convert_to_display.cc)).
- **OpenColorIO** (BSD-3-Clause) [V] ([AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO)). **ACES OCIO configs** (CG and Studio configs generated from aces-dev; New BSD; docs CC-BY-4.0) [V] ([OpenColorIO-Config-ACES](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO-Config-ACES)) (documentation/asset). The Natron fork recommends the ACES 2.0 studio config [V] ([fork README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Niik-l/Natron/master/README.md)).
- **Saffron** node groups add Oklab-based grading and view transforms (**flim**, Khronos PBR Neutral, ACES), and run in the viewport/realtime compositor. The README notes Blender's Render Layers output is "Linear BT.709 by default but it might change in future versions" [V] ([bean-mhm/Saffron](https://github.com/bean-mhm/Saffron); flim: [bean-mhm/flim](https://github.com/bean-mhm/flim)).
- Free LUT tooling found on GitHub (licenses not checked): [o-l-l-i/lut-maker](https://github.com/o-l-l-i/lut-maker) (interactive LUT maker); [ray-cast/lut](https://github.com/ray-cast/lut) (color-grading LUTs, .cube).

### Inferences
- **Recommended NPR setups for Blender 5.2 LTS [U]:**
  - *Flat/toon or hand-painted-texture look (Arcane-like albedo fidelity)*: View = **Standard**, Look = None. Keep lighting values within 0–1 for key areas. Do bloom/halation in comp. Grade after Convert to Display or in Resolve.
  - *Painterly lit look with hot practicals and sunsets (Mielgo-like overexposure)*: View = **AgX** (Base or Punchy) or **ACES 2.0** if delivering to ACES/HDR. Expect AgX to desaturate saturated highlights; compensate with Look/grade or render the toon parts under Standard via Convert to Display in a separate branch.
  - *Interchange*: write **EXR in Linear Rec.709** (or ACEScg if the studio pipeline requires it), and keep the same view transform in Resolve via the AgX DCTL or ACES config so comp and grade match.
- **Color script → final grade**: pre-production color keys are display-referred paintings. Matching them is easiest under Standard, or by inverting the chosen view. The 5.x config describes the "AgX Base sRGB" inverse space as usable "to convert backplates or matte paintings to the working space" [V], which is directly relevant for bringing painted backgrounds (Mielgo/Arcane style) into a scene-linear comp without a shift.
